# Meridian Programme — Business case summary

INVENTED TEST DOCUMENT — fictional organisation. Not a real business case.

Approved by University Executive Board, 20 January 2025
Version 1.0

## 1. Why the University is replacing Quill

Quill, the current student records system, has been in use since 1994. The supplier will end support on 31 December 2028. Quill cannot produce the data returns now required by the regulator without manual work, which takes the Registry about 140 staff days a year.

## 2. What the programme delivers

- A new student records system covering admissions, enrolment, assessment and awards.
- Migration of student records from Quill.
- Training for about 600 staff.

## 3. Timetable in this business case

| Stage | Planned date |
| --- | --- |
| Supplier appointed | March 2025 |
| Admissions go-live | November 2026 |
| Core records and enrolment go-live | September 2027 |
| Quill switched off | September 2028 |

## 4. Budget

The approved programme budget is £4,200,000 over three financial years.

| Budget line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Supplier implementation charges | £1,150,000 |
| University staff and backfill | £1,900,000 |
| Data migration and archive | £420,000 |
| Training and business change | £380,000 |
| Contingency | £350,000 |
| **Total** | **£4,200,000** |

Any service credits received from the supplier are returned to the contingency line. They may not be spent without the approval of the Programme Board.

Spending from contingency above £50,000 for a single item needs the approval of the Chief Operating Officer as well as the Programme Board.

## 5. Benefits

- Saving of 140 Registry staff days a year on regulator returns.
- Quill licence and hosting costs of £265,000 a year end when Quill is switched off.
- Applicants can track their application online.

The business case assumes the new system's annual charge will be £310,000. The net increase in annual running cost is therefore £45,000, which the Executive Board accepted because of the regulatory risk of staying on Quill.

## 6. Main risks at approval

1. Supplier delivery slips.
2. Data quality in Quill is worse than expected.
3. Staff do not complete training before go-live.
